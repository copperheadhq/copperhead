# Subsystem architecture

## Architecture scope and prose block diagram
Stage 2 of the fresh experimental create pipeline defines functional blocks and interfaces. docs/SPEC.md and its registered constraints remain authoritative in full; this document neither relaxes requirements nor claims verified component selection, electrical compliance, manufacturing readiness, or measured performance.

A documented adequate USB-C 5V source feeds the receptacle, ESD/short-circuit protection and controlled startup, then an efficient buck produces nominal 3.3V. That rail supplies the ESP32-C3 MCU, heater-disabled SHT40 sensor, external OLED, and remaining support loads. The MCU measures temperature/humidity over a shared 100kHz I2C bus and renders results or explicit errors on the OLED. Manual reset and boot controls plus an external 3.3V UART provide programming and logs. The MCU module's PCB antenna supports radio operation subject to power and RF checks. Ground is the common power/interface return; the four USB shell stakes connect directly to ground under the existing assumption. This describes functional connections only, not selected net names or refdes.

Rationale: expose subsystem dependencies before selecting packages, assigning pins, or drawing hardware.

## Power: protected USB-C input
Only USB-C 5V input is allowed, over 4.75–5.25V. There is no USB data, USB-PD, battery, mains, external voltage input, or intentional operation above 5V. Deployment requires explicit USB-C 1.5A/3A source advertisement or a separately verified adequate 5V source. Existing ASSUMED policy is documented source qualification pending a verified detection architecture; passive Rd does not detect the source-current advertisement or grant universal 500mA default-current entitlement.

Candidate USB4125-GF-A uses standard 1.00mm stakes: A5 CC1 and B5 CC2 each have their own 5.1k Rd to ground; they are never shorted. A9/B9 carry VBUS, A12/B12 are ground, and all four S1 plated shell stakes are grounded. Direct shell-to-ground is the existing ASSUMED implementation pending protection/EMC review. Later capture must verify the stock 6P symbol and exact standard footprint; pads/drills cannot be reduced. The 0190 variant has 1.90mm stakes and is not silently interchangeable.

Stage3 docs/BOM.md selects UNVERIFIED TPS259533DSGR for the adjustable current-limited protection block instead of the Stage2 TPS2553DBVR candidate; reason: explicit adjustable dVdt startup and greater transient-voltage headroom. R3=4.42k1%, C10=100nF, active-low EN grounded, proposed SMAJ6.0A VBUS TVS and TPD2E2U06 CC protection are selected for capture, subject to the BOM's full qualification conditions. Short-circuit protection, ESD, fault behavior and controlled startup are required. A nominal 500mA setting is neither a guaranteed hard cap nor permission to exceed the 450mA operating peak. Verify limit resistor/tolerance, switch drop/loss, capacitance, inrush, startup sequencing, protection leakage/quiescent current and fault behavior. No ESD test level is selected here.

Rationale: protect the input while keeping source entitlement and operating budgets distinct from a protection threshold.

## Power: 3.3V regulation and accounting
Use a buck rated at least 600mA. TPS62162DSGR is the existing fixed-3.3V, manufacturer-listed 1A candidate, not an accepted design. The rail must remain within 3.0–3.6V during startup, radio bursts and display loads. The existing ASSUMED efficiency acceptance floor is 75%, requiring verification across relevant input/load/thermal conditions. Verify exact package/pins, support components, stability, current limits, startup, ripple, transient droop and thermal behavior.

| Budget | Required value / interpretation |
|---|---|
| Input continuous | <=250mA, including display and protection overhead |
| Input peak | <=450mA, including startup/radio/display/protection overhead |
| MCU at 3.3V | <=150mA continuous; <=400mA transient |
| OLED at 3.3V | <=50mA allocation, not a vendor guarantee |
| Sensor at 3.3V | <=2mA allocation, heater disabled |
| Other 3.3V loads | <=20mA total |
| Simultaneous rail continuous | Derived 222mA = 150+50+2+20 |
| Simultaneous rail peak | Derived 472mA = 400+50+2+20 |

At 4.75V and 75% efficiency, the preliminary continuous conversion estimate is 3.3V*0.222A/(4.75V*0.75)=205.6mA, leaving approximately 44.4mA before upstream overhead. The peak estimate is 3.3V*0.472A/(4.75V*0.75)=437.2mA, leaving approximately 12.8mA before upstream overhead. These are arithmetic estimates, not evidence that the input targets are met. Protection drop changes the buck input conditions; reconcile losses, tolerances, startup/inrush and load timing without treating the margins as separate allocations or reducing any requirement. Peak duration remains unresolved.

Include every selected part's quiescent/leakage and all support loads in the appropriate rail or input budget; avoid double-counting currents already included in verified efficiency or load figures. Continuous operation is the existing ASSUMED usage model; no sleep-current allowance was supplied. No LDO heat claim is made. A later conflict must be reported, not hidden by lowering allocations or assuming non-simultaneous peaks.

Rationale: preserve the simultaneous load envelope and expose the narrow peak margin before committing a power circuit.

## Connectivity and shared I2C
The MCU, sensor and external OLED share a 100kHz I2C bus at 3.3V-compatible logic levels. SDA/SCL are functional descriptions, not assigned net names. Verify the exact SHT40 suffix/address and OLED 0x3C/0x3D configuration for no collision. Check module-installed pullups, effective parallel resistance, sink current, bus/cable capacitance and rise time before selecting additional pullups. No pullup value or intentional pullup omission is selected here.

The UART connects only to an external 3.3V-level programming/logging interface with labeled orientation and verified avoidance of target back-power. Radio behavior and firmware modes must stay inside the MCU current allocations. No wireless protocol or additional connector is selected.

Rationale: a shared slow bus serves both peripherals while keeping address, loading and cable effects explicit.

## Digital sensing
SHT40-AD1B-R2 is the existing candidate. Read temperature and humidity at 1Hz with the heater always off and sensor current within the 2mA allocation. Verify ordering suffix, supply compatibility, address, maximum operating/quiescent current, support circuit, conversion timing and CRC against manufacturer evidence. Place the sensor at least 10mm from MCU/regulator heat sources where practicable; report a placement conflict rather than silently waiving it.

A CRC or bus error invalidates the reading, produces an explicit display error, is logged through UART and is retried on the next scheduled sample under the existing ASSUMED error policy. Accuracy, humidity range and recovery-latency thresholds remain unspecified; none is invented here.

Rationale: heater-disabled periodic sensing preserves the current allocation and makes integrity failures visible.

## RF and mechanics
The board envelope is <=45x40mm with exactly two copper layers. Nominal thickness is 1.00mm to match the GCT standard-stake illustrated recommendation; thickness tolerance and physical fit remain unresolved. Hole-to-copper clearance is >=0.25mm with exact manufacturer/library pad and drill geometry retained. Shell/module/cable/enclosure fit must be checked; mounting and final outline are not selected. Ambient is 0–40C, non-condensing indoor sensing. Thermal verification must consider both component limits and sensor heating.

Put the module PCB antenna at a board edge, preferably extending beyond the base board. Enforce the manufacturer's keepout with no copper, traces or components on either layer. The additional 15mm in-plane clearance is the existing ASSUMED target pending Espressif hardware guidelines and actual board-shape reconciliation. Resolve conflicts among antenna clearance, heat separation, connector fit and the envelope explicitly. Layout remains draft quality.

Rationale: RF clearance and sensor thermal isolation constrain the architecture before placement, not after routing.
