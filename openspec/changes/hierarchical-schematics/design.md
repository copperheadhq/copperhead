# hierarchical-schematics: Design

## Context

The drafting engine (deterministic-schematic-drafting) emits one flat `.kicad_sch`: groups shelf-wrap into rows (its D12), cross-group nets are global net labels, and stage 4 iterates on the whole sheet. That design's own risk register names hierarchical sheets as an IR limitation deferred past v1, and the flat scope was right while designs stayed small. The lemondrop campaign (160 parts, `run-logs/2026-08-07T17-52-03`) is the counterexample: paper escalation chasing density, ~40k-token whole-sheet iteration context to fix one group, whole-sheet regressions discarding finished groups (R6/I16), and a router burdened by every other group's stub columns (#204 class). The banding fix (de5aa02) relieves paper escalation but not iteration cost, blast radius, or human reviewability past ~200 parts.

The structural observation from #207: hierarchy is already in the data. Every non-power part names a `group` validated against SUBSYSTEMS.md, and the wiring policy already computes the in-group versus cross-group split (wires versus labels). A child-sheet partition and its sheet-pin sets are therefore derivable with zero new model-authored input; the IR is unchanged.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- One child sheet per SUBSYSTEMS.md group plus a derived top sheet, from the same IR, with per-file byte-identical regeneration.
- Stage 4 iterates one sheet at a time: bounded context, findings scoped to a sheet, each clean sheet separately committable, no cross-sheet blast radius.
- Gates keep their meaning: legibility/score/wire-contact per sheet, ERC once across the hierarchy, drift sheet-qualified.
- Flat emission unchanged below the threshold; existing goldens and AC-16 pins keep passing in flat mode.

**Non-Goals:**

- Hierarchy deeper than one level (top sheet plus children).
- Partitions other than SUBSYSTEMS.md groups (no model-chosen or size-balanced partitioning).
- Sheet reuse/multi-instantiation (each child sheet is instantiated exactly once, so refdes stay globally unique and BOM/drift semantics are untouched).
- Editing human-drawn hierarchies; the engine only drafts hierarchies it authors.

## Decisions

- **H1: The partition is the IR's `group` field; the IR schema does not change.** One child sheet per SUBSYSTEMS.md group, named `<project>-<slug>.kicad_sch` where the slug is the group heading sanitized by the same token rule the emitter already uses for generated power symbols. Sheet order on the top sheet is the group flow order the flat engine already computes (directed pin-type graph, SUBSYSTEMS.md declaration order as tie-break), shelf-wrapped like D12. No new authoring surface means the model's repair levers stay exactly the IR levers D5/D6a established.

- **H2: Hierarchical is a mode, chosen deterministically, with flat as the default.** The engine drafts hierarchically when the IR's placed part count exceeds a configured threshold (`legibility.hierarchyThreshold`, documented default) or when `hints.hierarchy` demands it; `hints.hierarchy: "flat"` forces flat regardless of size. The decision is stated in the draft report. Flat mode's behavior, goldens, and AC-16 pins are untouched, so this change is additive at the contract level even though it modifies the drafting requirements' text: below the threshold nothing moves by a single grid unit.

- **H3: Cross-group nets become hierarchical labels on child sheets and sheet pins on the top sheet.** The cross-group net set is already computed by the wiring policy; in hierarchical mode each such net gets a hierarchical label in the child sheet's stub column (same placement mechanics as today's global labels) and a matching sheet pin on that group's sheet symbol. Pin side follows the label's stub side; pins sort deterministically (by side, then name). In-group nets, reductions, power symbols, decoupling rows, and the alignment pass are unchanged per sheet: a child sheet is drafted by the existing per-group pipeline with its group as the only group on the sheet. Nets local to one group never appear on the top sheet.

- **H4: The top sheet is derived, never authored.** One sheet symbol per group sized from its pin count, title block filled from the same source as today, no components. Top-sheet wiring between sheet symbols is deliberately omitted in v1: KiCad connects same-named sheet pins through the hierarchy without drawn top-sheet wires only when labels are global, so child-sheet cross-group labels that must resolve through the top sheet get short stub wires with net labels at each sheet pin, generated by the same stub mechanics as child sheets. This keeps the top sheet readable (a block diagram) while staying electrically sound under hierarchical ERC.

- **H5: Determinism extends per file via sheet-qualified UUIDv5 paths.** Semantic paths gain the sheet as their first segment (`<sheet>/R1`, `<sheet>/wire/<net>/<index>`, `top/sheet/<group>`, `top/sheet/<group>/pin/<net>`); each emitted file keeps canonical section order and formatting. Identical IR yields a byte-identical file set; editing one group's members re-emits only that child sheet and, when its cross-group net set changed, the top sheet. Symbol `(instances …)` project paths are part of the template and derive from the top sheet's UUID, which is itself UUIDv5-stable, so annotation data cannot wobble between drafts.

- **H6: Stage 4 loops per sheet; the IR stays one document.** The stage drafts the full hierarchy once, then iterates: pick the first sheet with error-severity findings, repair through the IR, re-draft (H5 makes untouched sheets byte-stable, so a per-sheet regression is visible as exactly that sheet's diff), commit when the sheet is clean. The completion contract requires every sheet clean, the hierarchy drift-clean and matching a re-draft of the current IR, and one hierarchy-wide ERC pass at the end. Because clean sheets are committed as they land, a later regression rolls back one sheet's diff, not the stage: this is the structural R6/I16 fix, and it needs no new ledger machinery because the existing obligations already key on findings, which are now sheet-scoped.

- **H7: Gates keep one meaning each.** The legibility checker already walks hierarchies and attributes findings per sheet (its "Every sheet in a hierarchy is checked" requirement); it needs only the top-sheet vocabulary (sheet symbols and sheet pins participate in overlap/off-grid/out-of-frame like symbols and labels, and the child-sheet group-box rule treats the sheet's single group normally). The scorer computes per-sheet composites plus a part-count-weighted aggregate, all breakdowns reported; error findings on any sheet cap the aggregate. ERC runs once from the root sheet, since `kicad-cli` resolves hierarchies natively; per-sheet ERC would double-report cross-sheet nets. The drift checker reports sheet-qualified paths so a BOM/PINOUT divergence names the sheet that owns the part.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Two emission modes double the surface the goldens must hold] → the child-sheet pipeline is the existing per-group pipeline, not a fork; hierarchical Tier C goldens pin the file set byte-exactly; flat goldens stay pinned unchanged, proving H2's "nothing moves below the threshold".
- [KiCad hierarchical semantics (instances blocks, sheet-pin/label matching, global versus hierarchical label scope) are subtler than flat emission] → v1 confines version-specific hierarchy tokens to the emitter templates, the read-only parser cross-checks emitted connectivity across the hierarchy against the IR's net list, and hierarchy-wide ERC in CI is the external referee.
- [A group larger than the largest paper cannot become one child sheet] → the banding pass (de5aa02) already handles oversized groups within a sheet; a group that still cannot fit fails validation with a named finding (split the group in SUBSYSTEMS.md), matching D6's fail-early philosophy.
- [Per-sheet commits interleave with the sync-obligations ledger mid-stage] → committing a clean sheet requires that sheet's obligations closed, not the stage's; the stage-level contract still holds everything at the end. No obligation kind is added or weakened.
- [The threshold creates a discontinuity: one part added flips a design to hierarchical] → the flip is deterministic, stated in the draft report, and overridable both ways via `hints.hierarchy`; goldens pin both modes so the flip itself is regression-tested.

## Migration Plan

Sequenced after `deterministic-schematic-drafting` archives (this change modifies its requirements) and after the first end-to-end green run on the flat pipeline (#207's own sequencing: flat sheets are not the current blocker, and hierarchical goldens landing mid-campaign would churn the corpus). Implementation is additive behind the mode decision: emitter templates and sheet-qualified UUIDs first (flat mode byte-identical before and after, proven by unchanged goldens), then hierarchical drafting and the top sheet as `copperhead draft schematic` output, then per-sheet scoring and drift, then the stage-4 per-sheet loop last. Rollback is the mode switch: forcing `hints.hierarchy: "flat"` (or a threshold of infinity) restores current behavior with no code revert.

## Open Questions

- Default `hierarchyThreshold`: the lemondrop evidence says 160 parts is past it and attempt-05 says a flat A1 at that scale is workable; somewhere in 80-120 placed parts is the likely default, to be settled against corpus data.
- Whether the top sheet should also carry a generated block-diagram annotation (net names on the stub wires may suffice; a legend may read better past ~10 groups).
- Whether `copperhead draft schematic` needs a `--sheet <group>` selector for partial re-drafts, or whether whole-hierarchy drafting with byte-stable untouched sheets (H5) is always cheap enough.
